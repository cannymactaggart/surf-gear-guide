BOARD BUYING

Don't buy a surfboard until you've worked out these 5 things.

The right board depends on the surfer, the waves and how you actually intend to use it.

01 — YOUR EXPERIENCE

How consistently can you actually stand and catch waves?

Be realistic about your current ability. A board that is technically impressive can be a poor choice if it makes catching waves and maintaining balance unnecessarily difficult.

Beginners and newer surfers generally need stability and easy paddling. If you are already catching waves consistently, the next board can be chosen around the skills you are trying to develop rather than simply being smaller.

02 — VOLUME

How much flotation makes sense?

Volume is measured in litres and gives you a useful starting point for comparing boards, but it is not the only specification that matters.

Two boards with similar volume can feel very different because width, thickness, outline and rocker change how that volume is distributed. Treat volume as one part of the decision rather than a magic number.

03 — WAVES

What are the waves at your usual break actually like?

A board suited to small, weak summer waves may not be the same board you want for powerful beach breaks or clean point waves.

Think about the conditions you will ride most often, not the best surf you might encounter a few times a year.

04 — SIZE AND SHAPE

Don't choose by length alone.

Length, width, thickness, outline, rocker and rails all affect how a board paddles, turns and feels underfoot.

Compare the complete dimensions and intended use. A shorter, wider board can have substantially more usable stability than a longer, narrow performance shape.

05 — BUDGET

What can you spend without compromising the rest of your kit?

A board is not the whole cost of surfing. You may also need fins, a leash, traction or wax, and appropriate protection from the conditions.

A cheaper board that suits you can be a better purchase than an expensive board chosen for its reputation or appearance.

Bottom line: choose for the surfer and the waves you actually have, not the surfer you hope to become next year.
