PROGRESSION

You've outgrown your foamie. What's actually next?

Moving on does not automatically mean buying the smallest hard board you can find.

Look at what you can do now

How consistently do you catch waves? Can you angle your take-off, trim and turn? The answers matter more than simply how long you've owned your foamie.

Consider a gradual step

A larger hard board, funboard or mid-length can reduce size without removing all the stability and paddle power you have learned to rely on.

Match the next board to your waves

Small, weak waves may favour a different shape from steep beach breaks. Choose around the conditions you actually surf.

Don't confuse progression with going smaller

A board that lets you catch more waves can help you improve faster than a difficult board chosen because it looks more advanced.

Useful test: if the new board dramatically reduces the number of waves you can catch, it may be too big a jump.
