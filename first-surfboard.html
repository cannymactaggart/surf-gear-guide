BOARDS

Don't buy a surfboard until you've worked out these things.

The right board is less about what looks good in the shop and more about who is riding it, where they're surfing and what they want to do.

What sort of surfer are you?

If you're starting out, stability and paddle power are usually more useful than a board that looks like the one your favourite pro rides. A bigger board can make it easier to catch waves and get your balance. That doesn't mean you need the biggest board you can find.

If you've already got some experience, think about what you want the new board to do that your current one doesn't. Catch smaller waves? Turn more easily? Handle better waves? That is a much better starting point than simply deciding you want a shorter board.

Your size matters

Height and weight both affect how much board you need. Volume is useful here because it gives you an idea of how much flotation the board has, but there isn't a magic litres-per-kilo number that works for everyone.

Two boards with the same volume can feel completely different. Width, thickness, outline and where the volume sits all change how a board paddles and rides.

What waves will you actually surf?

This is easy to overlook. A board that works brilliantly in clean, powerful waves may be frustrating in small, weak beach-break waves. If most of your surfing is in ordinary Australian beach breaks, choose something that works in those conditions rather than buying for the best day of the year.

Board types in plain English

Foamies are stable, forgiving and a good way to learn. They're not just children's boards, either.

Funboards and mid-lengths sit between a beginner board and a performance shortboard. They give you some stability without feeling like a barge.

Longboards are long, stable and can catch small waves easily. They are also a completely legitimate choice for an experienced surfer who likes the way they ride.

Shortboards are generally less stable and need more speed and technique. They're designed for manoeuvrability rather than making learning easy.

Length isn't everything

Two boards can be the same length and behave nothing alike. Width, thickness, rocker, rails and the outline all matter. Volume matters too, but it shouldn't be the only number you look at.

And then there's the budget

Remember that the board is only part of the cost. You'll probably need fins and a leash, and possibly traction, wax, a board bag or other bits and pieces. A cheaper board can stop being cheap once you've added everything else.

The short version: buy the board that makes it easier for you to catch waves and enjoy surfing. The most advanced board isn't automatically the best one.

Thinking about moving on from a foamie?

Don't assume the next step has to be a shortboard. A funboard, mid-length or larger hybrid can be a much more useful transition, depending on your size, ability and the waves you surf.
