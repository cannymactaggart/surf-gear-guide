ACCESSORIES

The bits you don't think about until you need them.

Some surf gear is essential. Some just makes getting to the beach and back a lot easier.

Leashes

A leash is essential on most boards. Match its length and thickness to the board and the conditions you're surfing. It needs to be long enough to let the board move without creating unnecessary drag or getting in the way.

Board bags

A board bag is useful even if you don't travel by plane. It protects the board from knocks in the car, on roof racks and while you're carrying it around. For everyday use, you don't necessarily need the most heavily padded travel bag.

Key safes

If your car key can't go in the water with you, a secure key safe can save you from hiding it under a wheel or hoping nobody notices it sitting on a tyre. Just make sure the safe is actually designed for outdoor use and is attached somewhere sensible.

Wax and traction

You need some way of keeping your feet attached to the board. Depending on the board and conditions, that might mean wax, a traction pad or both. Check what the board is designed for before covering it in something it doesn't need.

Changing mats and beach gear

A changing mat isn't essential, but once you've tried standing on wet sand while pulling a wetsuit off, it starts to make a lot more sense. The same goes for a decent towel, changing robe or somewhere to put wet gear in the car.

Roof-rack straps

If you're carrying boards on a car, use proper surfboard straps and make sure the board is secured correctly. It's not the sort of thing worth improvising.

Ding repair

A small repair kit can be handy for minor damage, particularly if you're away from a surf shop. It isn't a substitute for getting a serious ding properly repaired, but it can stop a small problem becoming a waterlogged board.

What do you actually need? Start with the essentials for the board you're riding. The rest can be added when you find yourself repeatedly wishing you had it.
