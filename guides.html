BUYING GUIDES

Make the expensive decisions a little easier.

Straightforward explanations of the surf gear decisions that can otherwise lead to expensive mistakes.

BOARD BASICS

Don't buy a surfboard until you've worked out these 5 things

Before comparing brands or chasing a bargain, work out who the board is for and where it will be used.

1. Your ability

A board that is easy to paddle and stable enough to catch waves is usually more useful to a beginner than a smaller performance shape.

2. Your size

Your height and weight affect the volume and dimensions that make sense. There is no single ideal board length for everyone.

3. The waves

Small, weak waves call for different characteristics from powerful, hollow waves. Be realistic about the conditions you actually surf.

4. Board type

Foam boards, funboards, mid-lengths and shortboards each make different trade-offs between stability, manoeuvrability and ease of use.

5. Your budget

Leave room for the rest of the setup: fins, leash, traction or wax, and potentially a wetsuit.

PROGRESSION

You've outgrown your foamie. What's actually next?

Moving on does not automatically mean buying the smallest hard board you can find. The useful question is what will let you catch plenty of waves while continuing to improve.

A larger hard board, funboard or mid-length can provide a sensible step down in size without removing all the stability and paddle power you've already learned to rely on.

Consider your current board, the waves you surf, how consistently you catch waves and what you want to improve before choosing the next shape.

FINS

FCS, FCS II or Futures? Don't buy the wrong fins

Fin boxes are not one universal standard. The fin system on the board determines which fins you can use.

FCS and FCS II are related systems but are not simply interchangeable in every situation, while Futures uses a different base and box design. Check the manufacturer's specification or the board's listing before buying.

If buying second-hand, inspect both the fins and the boxes for cracks, damage or repairs before paying.

BUDGET

How much should you actually spend on your first surfboard?

There is no useful universal dollar figure. The right budget depends on whether you are buying new or second-hand, the board type and what else you need to buy.

A cheap board that is unsuitable for your size or ability can be poor value. Conversely, paying for a premium performance board makes little sense if you are still learning the basics.

Compare the complete setup rather than the board price alone.

SECOND-HAND

Buying a second-hand surfboard? Here's what to check

Look closely at the rails, nose, tail, bottom and fin boxes. Dings and repairs are not automatically deal-breakers, but water intrusion and poorly repaired structural damage can be.

Check for soft spots, major dents, cracks around fin boxes and signs that the board has taken on water. Ask what repairs have been done and, if possible, inspect them in good light.

Also check that the dimensions and volume actually suit you. A low price does not compensate for a board you cannot comfortably ride.

WETSUITS

What wetsuit do you actually need in Australia?

The answer depends on location, season, water temperature, wind and how long you normally stay in the water.

Thickness is only part of the decision. A well-fitting suit can be more useful than simply choosing the thickest option available, and different designs trade warmth against flexibility.

Check local water temperatures for the area and season you actually surf rather than relying on a generic Australian recommendation.
