SURFING DOGS

Some dogs really do surf.

And Noosa has turned it into one of the most entertaining events on the Australian surf calendar.

The Noosa Festival of Surfing's Surfing Dog Championships have been running since 2012. What started as a playful addition to the festival has become a major crowd-puller, with thousands of people turning up to watch dogs and their humans take on the waves at First Point.

The 2026 event was the 15th championship. Twelve dogs competed, and the Best Wave award went to Loui, a Border Collie surfing with Rodrigo. Basil the Corgi took Best Wipeout, while Bu, the Blue English Staffy, was the Crowd Favourite.

It isn't just a dressed-up photo opportunity either. The dogs are actually judged on their surfing, and the event has developed its own collection of regulars and characters over the years.

Watch the dogs in action

One of Noosa's crowd favourites

Rama, a rescue dog who surfed with Chris de Aboitiz for 14 years, became one of the best-known dogs at the festival. Rama retired from competition in 2025, with Chris continuing to surf with the next generation of his dogs.

That's one of the things that makes the Noosa event more interesting than a novelty act: some of the dogs become experienced surfers with years of time on the board.

Want to give surfing a go with your little mate?

Start with the dog, not the gear. Some dogs love the water and some really don't. Keep the first attempts easy, stay in conditions you're comfortable with and don't force it if your dog isn't enjoying it.

A properly fitted dog life jacket is worth considering, particularly one with a handle that makes it easier to help your dog out of the water. It doesn't turn a dog into a swimmer, though, so keep an eye on them the whole time.

If your furry pal turns out to be a natural, you're going to want some evidence. A dog-friendly camera harness such as the GoPro Fetch can let you capture the action without trying to hold a camera at the same time.

And if a little encouragement helps with the training, use whatever small treat your dog already loves. Carrot works for some dogs; there are also plenty of ordinary dog training treats that are easy to carry to the beach.

Keep it sensible

Conditions that are manageable for a person can still be a lot for a dog. Watch the surf, currents, temperature and your dog's behaviour. Rinse salt and sand off afterwards and make sure your dog has fresh water available.
