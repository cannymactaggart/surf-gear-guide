WETSUITS

What wetsuit do you actually need in Australia?

The answer depends on where and when you surf, how long you stay in the water and how cold you actually get.

Start with where you surf

Australia has a lot of coastline and the water temperature isn't the same everywhere. A suit that makes sense in winter in Victoria is a very different proposition from one you might wear in summer in Queensland.

Check the usual water temperature for your local break and the season you actually surf. If you're travelling, check that too.

What do 2/2, 3/2 and 4/3 actually mean?

The numbers refer to the thickness of the neoprene, usually with the first number describing the thicker panels and the second the thinner ones. A 3/2 is therefore generally warmer than a 2/2, while a 4/3 is warmer again.

More thickness isn't automatically better. It adds warmth but can also add weight and reduce flexibility. You want enough suit to stay comfortable, not the thickest one in the shop.

Full suit, springsuit or just a top?

A full suit covers your arms and legs and is the obvious choice when the water is properly cold. A springsuit leaves the arms or legs partly exposed and can be useful when you need some warmth without a full winter suit.

A rash vest or wetsuit top can be enough in warmer water, particularly if you're mainly trying to avoid sun, wind or a bit of chill.

Fit matters more than people think

A wetsuit should feel close and snug without making it difficult to breathe, paddle or move your shoulders. You don't want big loose areas where water can constantly flush through, but you also shouldn't be fighting the suit every time you paddle.

Try it on before buying if you can. Different brands and cuts suit different bodies, even when the stated size is the same.

Chest zip, back zip or zip-free?

Chest-zip and zip-free suits can give a good seal around the neck and tend to be popular with surfers who don't want a big zip across the back. Back-zip suits are usually easier to get into, which is not a trivial advantage when you're standing in a cold car park trying to get changed.

When is spending more worth it?

Better neoprene and construction can make a noticeable difference in warmth, flexibility and durability. But you don't need the most expensive suit just because it's the newest one.

If you're surfing once a month in warm water, your priorities are different from someone who is in the water several times a week all winter.

The short version: choose for your local water, your season and how long you actually spend surfing. Get the fit right, then worry about the logo.
